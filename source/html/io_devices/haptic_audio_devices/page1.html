Introduction to Audio & Haptic Interfaces

Visual displays have long dominated user interfaces, but hearing and touch matter just as much in human-computer interaction. Audio and haptic devices connect digital data to what we can actually hear and feel.

By Francesco Corti 2 min read

Beyond the Screen: Multi-Sensory Computing

Audio interfaces let machines communicate through sound and let people control them by voice. Haptic interfaces, from the Greek haptikos (“able to touch or grasp”), create an experience of touch by applying forces, vibrations or motions to the user [1][2].

Both began with very simple signals, a beep or a buzz. Over the decades they grew into systems that recognise speech, place sounds in three-dimensional space and let you feel a virtual object.
