Alternative & Niche I/O Devices: Introduction

Some devices are not made for everyone. They are designed for one task, one environment or one user, and that is what makes them niche.

By Francesco Corti 2 min read

What makes a device “alternative” or “niche”?

The keyboard, mouse and screen are built for the average user doing general-purpose work. An alternative or niche I/O device is the opposite: it is designed for one specific task, one specific environment or one specific user. It trades the flexibility of a general-purpose device for a much better fit in the situation it was made for.

A flight stick suits a task, a rugged factory terminal suits an environment, and an adaptive controller suits a user with limited mobility. Most of these devices are rare in everyday life, yet many of their ideas later reached the mainstream.

What you will find in the next pages

  • Gaming & simulation: flight-style controls and omnidirectional treadmills.
  • Controllers for professionals and creatives: 3D navigation devices and MIDI instruments.
  • Industry and harsh environments: operator panels and touchscreens that work with gloves, dust and vibration.
  • Assistive and research devices: adaptive controllers, eye tracking and sip-and-puff switches.
