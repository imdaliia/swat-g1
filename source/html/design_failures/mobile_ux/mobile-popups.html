Intrusive Mobile Pop-ups and Ads

When advertisements interrupt the mobile browsing experience.

By Emilie Bui 5 min read

Introduction

Online advertisements are a common part of the internet. They allow websites to generate revenue and promote products or services. However, when advertisements interrupt users or cover important content, they can become a serious usability problem.

This problem is particularly noticeable on mobile devices. Smartphone screens are smaller than computer screens, so even a relatively small pop-up can cover a large part of the visible content. Users may have to close several windows before they can start reading.

Intrusive advertisements can therefore turn a simple browsing experience into a frustrating one. Instead of focusing on the information they came to find, users are forced to interact with promotional content. Google describes intrusive interstitials as elements that obstruct users' view of the main content. [1]

What Are Intrusive Pop-ups and Ads?

A pop-up is a window or visual element that appears over the main content of a website. Some pop-ups provide useful information, such as cookie settings or important notifications. However, promotional pop-ups become intrusive when they prevent users from accessing the content they want to see.

There are several types of intrusive advertising. Full-screen interstitials cover almost the entire page and require users to close them before continuing. Floating advertisements remain visible while users scroll, sometimes covering text or interactive elements. Banners can also become disruptive when they occupy too much screen space.

Another problem is the frequency of advertisements. When pop-ups appear repeatedly or immediately after users close them, they interrupt the natural flow of navigation and make it harder to concentrate. [3]

1. Red Hot Cyber

Red Hot Cyber provides an example of a promotional pop-up appearing over an article. Instead of allowing visitors to immediately read the information, the website displays an additional promotional window.

The problem

A promotional message appears over the article and interrupts the reading experience. Users cannot comfortably access the underlying content until they dismiss the pop-up.

Why does it fail?

  • Important content is partially or completely hidden.
  • Users are interrupted before they can read the article.
  • The pop-up takes attention away from the main information.
  • Closing the window adds an unnecessary action.

How could it be improved?

The website could replace the large pop-up with a smaller promotional banner. This would allow users to see the advertisement without blocking the article. If a pop-up is necessary, its close button should be visible and easy to use.

2. Multiplayer.it

Multiplayer.it provides another example of how several advertising elements can compete for attention on the same mobile screen. In the screenshot, advertisements include a floating video and banners that cover parts of the article. [2]

The problem

Several advertisements compete with the main content for space and attention. The article becomes harder to read because advertisements occupy areas that should remain available to users.

Why does it fail?

  • Multiple advertisements create visual clutter.
  • Floating videos can cover important information.
  • Small close buttons can be difficult to select.
  • Users may accidentally interact with advertisements.
  • The main content becomes less important visually.

How could it be improved?

The website could reduce the number of advertisements displayed at the same time. It could also avoid placing floating elements over paragraphs and ensure that advertisements remain clearly separated from the article.

How Do Intrusive Ads Affect User Experience?

The main purpose of a website is to help visitors access information or complete a task. When advertisements constantly interrupt this process, the quality of the user experience decreases.

  • Content obstruction: Important information is hidden behind promotional elements.
  • Interruption: Users have to stop reading to close advertisements.
  • Visual overload: Too many advertisements compete for the user's attention.
  • Accidental interactions: Small close buttons and overlapping elements can lead to unwanted clicks.
  • Loss of trust: Repeated interruptions can make visitors less willing to return to the website.

These problems can also affect accessibility. Users with visual or motor impairments may have additional difficulty identifying or closing promotional windows. If the main content is covered, accessing information can become unnecessarily complicated.

Why Is This Also an Accessibility Problem?

Digital accessibility means that websites should remain usable by people with different abilities. An intrusive pop-up can create barriers when it is difficult to identify, understand or dismiss.

For example, a user navigating with a keyboard should be able to reach the close button and return to the main content. Users relying on assistive technologies should also be able to understand that a new dialog has appeared and how to interact with it.

A well-designed dialog should have a clear purpose, understandable information and an accessible way to close it. Promotional content should not prevent users from reaching the information they originally requested. [1]

How Can These Problems Be Prevented?

Advertising does not necessarily have to disappear from websites. The objective is to find a balance between business needs and the user's ability to access content comfortably.

Google recommends using smaller banners instead of full-page promotional interstitials because they allow users to access the main content without first dismissing a window. [1]

  • Use smaller banners: Avoid covering the entire screen with promotional messages.
  • Make close buttons visible: Users should immediately understand how to dismiss an advertisement.
  • Limit advertising frequency: Avoid displaying pop-ups repeatedly during the same browsing session.
  • Protect the main content: Keep important text and navigation accessible at all times.
  • Separate ads from content: Make it clear which elements are advertisements and which are part of the article.
  • Consider mobile screens: Check that advertisements do not occupy too much of the available space.

Conclusion

Intrusive pop-ups and advertisements are examples of design failures because they interrupt users and make it harder to access the information they are looking for. These problems become especially noticeable on smartphones, where screen space is limited.

A good mobile experience should balance advertising with usability and accessibility. By using smaller banners, clear controls and fewer interruptions, websites can promote their products without compromising the browsing experience.
