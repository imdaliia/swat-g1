Information Architecture and Navigation

Before deciding what an interface looks like, we have to decide where everything goes.

Designing interfaces By Alessandro Campanari

A familiar problem

Have you ever looked for an exam date on a university website and tried Courses, Students and Calendar before giving up? The information was there, but the structure hid it.

Key idea

People cannot use what they cannot find.

What information architecture is

Information architecture (IA) is the practice of organizing and labeling content so that people can find what they need and know where they are. Like the plan of a building, it is invisible, but everyone notices when it is wrong.

"The structural design of shared information environments."

Rosenfeld, Morville and Arango, Information Architecture

It comes right after the requirements: once we know what people need to do, IA decides where each thing lives.

Thinking like a supermarket

Nobody reads a manual before shopping, yet most people find the milk in a minute. A supermarket uses the same four systems as a website.

The four systems of IA
System In a supermarket In a course catalogue
Organization Products grouped in aisles Courses grouped by semester and subject
Labeling The signs above the aisles Menu items such as "Courses" or "Enrolment"
Navigation The store map The menu, breadcrumbs and links
Search Asking a shop assistant The search box and its filters

Matching the user's mental model

Users expect information to be organized in a certain way. A common mistake is to copy the structure of the organization instead of following the users' tasks.

Organized like the university

Student Office, Faculty Secretariat, Academic Services.

Organized like the student

Find a course, Enrol, Exams and grades.

How an information architecture is designed

  1. Study users and content
  2. Group and label
  3. Test with users
  4. Design the navigation
Three questions for every structure
  1. Can users predict where information is?
  2. Are the categories clear to them?
  3. Can they recover from a wrong path?
About accessibility

A clear structure also helps people who use screen readers or the keyboard. The details are in the Web accessibility topic.
