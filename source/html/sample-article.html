A machine without pedals

In 1817 the German inventor Karl Drais rode a strange wooden machine through Mannheim. It had two wheels in a line, a seat and a handlebar, but no pedals: the rider pushed against the ground with their feet, like a child on a balance bike[1].

Drais called it the Laufmaschine, the "running machine". It was fast on good roads and useless on bad ones, but it proved one important idea: a person can keep their balance on two wheels while moving.

Life is like riding a bicycle. To keep your balance you must keep moving.

- Albert Einstein, letter to his son Eduard (1930)

Timeline

  1. 1817 Karl Drais presents the running machine, pushed with the feet.
  2. 1860s In France, pedals are fixed to the front wheel. In English this machine is nicknamed the "boneshaker".
  3. 1870s The high-wheeler, or penny-farthing, makes the front wheel huge to go further with each turn of the pedals.
  4. 1885 John Kemp Starley's Rover "safety bicycle" uses two similar wheels and a chain to the rear wheel[2].
  5. 1888 John Boyd Dunlop patents the pneumatic tyre, and the ride stops shaking your bones[2].

Four designs compared

How each design moved the rider
Design Year Drive Main problem
Running machine 1817 Feet on the ground Needs smooth roads
Boneshaker 1860s Pedals on the front wheel Heavy and uncomfortable
Penny-farthing 1870s Pedals on a huge front wheel Dangerous falls
Safety bicycle 1885 Chain to the rear wheel Solved
Penny-farthing
Named after two British coins of different sizes, like its two wheels.
Safety bicycle
The name says it all: compared with the penny-farthing, its rider sat low and could put a foot down.

A lesson in design

The penny-farthing was fast, but a small bump could throw the rider over the handlebar. The safety bicycle won because it fixed the problem for the person, not for the machine: same speed thanks to the chain, but a rider who could reach the ground.

Good practice

Test a design with the people who will use it. Many problems of the penny-farthing were obvious only after a fall.

Be careful

The oldest design is not always the worst one. Today's balance bikes for children are basically Drais's running machine.

Questions

Why was the front wheel of the penny-farthing so big?

The pedals were fixed to the wheel, so one turn of the pedals meant one turn of the wheel. A bigger wheel covered more ground with every turn.

What made the safety bicycle safer?

The chain let the designers make the wheels small without losing speed. The rider sat lower, behind the front wheel, and was much less likely to fall over the handlebar.
