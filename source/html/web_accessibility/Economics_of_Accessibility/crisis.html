The PC Accessibility Crisis

Analyzing the impact of AI-driven component inflation on consumer hardware and market contraction.

By Pollazzon Manuel 6 min read

PC Accessibility Crisis

Abstract

Since late 2025, the global technology sector has faced an acute memory crisis characterized by explosive price inflation in Dynamic Random Access Memory (DRAM). Driven by enterprise demand for High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and server DRAM in Artificial Intelligence (AI) data centers, major manufacturers have reallocated manufacturing capacity away from consumer hardware. This paper synthesizes market statistics from Gartner, GSMA, and ITU to analyze the structural mechanics of component cost inflation, its impact on entry-level PC and smartphone markets, and the resulting widening of the global digital divide.

Introduction and Component Inflation

The semiconductor industry experienced a fundamental shift beginning in October 2025. Unprecedented capital expenditure in AI infrastructure created an insatiable demand for HBM and enterprise-grade server DRAM. A single AI server requires between 8 and 10 times the memory capacity of a standard enterprise server, incentivizing memory fabricators to prioritize high-margin enterprise orders over consumer-grade DDR5 components.

Contract pricing for standard DDR5 16Gb chips surged from $8.70 in October 2025 to $48.00 in September 2026—representing a price surge of over 450% (with spot market spikes reaching up to 600%). According to market research from Gartner, PC memory expenses are projected to reach a peak of 23% of the total bill-of-materials (BOM) in 2026, up from 16% in 2025. Combined DRAM and SSD cost increases are expected to reach 130% by the end of 2026.

Market Contraction and Segment Erasure

The rapid rise in component costs has severely eroded margins for budget hardware manufacturers. Vendors are increasingly unable to absorb component inflation, leading directly to the contraction of consumer shipments and the extinction of low-cost hardware tiers.

Key global market impact and forecast metrics (2026–2028) show a DDR5 16Gb contract price increase of +451% ($8.70 to $48.00) and a peak memory share of PC BOM in 2026 reaching 23% (up from 16% in 2025). This has driven a global PC shipment change (2026 vs 2025) of −10.4% and a global smartphone shipment change of −8.4%. Consumers are facing an average PC retail price increase of +17% and an average smartphone retail price increase of +13%. According to the Gartner forecast, the extinction horizon for the sub-$500 entry PC market is set for the Year 2028.

Gartner explicitly predicts that the sub-$500 entry-level PC market will disappear by 2028, as manufacturers pivot exclusively toward premium and enterprise offerings. Consequently, worldwide PC shipments are expected to shrink by 10.4% and smartphone shipments by 8.4% in 2026. To adapt to higher replacement costs, business users are extending PC lifespans by an average of 15%, while consumers are delaying upgrades by 20%.
